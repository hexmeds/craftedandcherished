If you want a simple bracelet project that looks colorful and impressive without using complicated knots, this **spiral friendship bracelet** is a great place to start.

This pattern uses embroidery floss and one easy repeating knot. As you work, the bracelet begins to twist naturally, creating the spiral effect without any special shaping. You can make it with two colors, three colors, or even five colors depending on the look you want.

It is a beginner-friendly project that works well for gifts, friendship bracelets, summer crafts, and quick handmade accessories.

## What Is a Spiral Friendship Bracelet?

A spiral friendship bracelet is made by repeatedly tying the same type of knot around the remaining strands.

Instead of alternating sides to keep the bracelet flat, you keep working with one color at a time. Because the knots are made repeatedly in the same direction, the bracelet starts to twist naturally.

Then you switch to another color and repeat the same process.

This creates colorful sections that wrap around the bracelet in a spiral.

## Why This Bracelet Is Great for Beginners

This is a simple project because you do not need to learn several complicated knotting techniques.

You mainly need to remember one sequence:

  * separate one working color
  * cross it over the other strands
  * pass it underneath
  * pull it through
  * tighten
  * repeat



Once you understand the first color section, the rest of the bracelet follows the same method.

It is also easy to customize because you can change:

  * the number of colors
  * the size of each color section
  * the overall bracelet length
  * the order of the colors



## Materials You Will Need

To make this spiral friendship bracelet, you will need:

 

![Materials for making an easy spiral friendship bracelet including embroidery floss, scissors, tape, and ruler](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/spiral-friendship-bracelet-materials.webp)

 

  * embroidery floss
  * scissors
  * ruler or measuring tape
  * tape
  * flat work surface



You can choose as many embroidery floss colors as you like.

For a beginner project, **2 to 5 colors** works well.

This tutorial uses **3 colors**.

### Choosing Your Colors

You can use:

  * three contrasting colors
  * three pastel colors
  * rainbow shades
  * school colors
  * holiday colors
  * two shades of the same color
  * neutral colors for a more minimal look



Using three clearly different colors can make it easier to follow the pattern while learning.

## Safety Note

Be careful when using scissors.

Cut away from your hands and fingers, especially when trimming the embroidery floss at the end of the bracelet.

# How to Make a Spiral Friendship Bracelet

## Step 1: Measure Your Wrist

Start by measuring around your wrist with a ruler or measuring tape.

Take that measurement and:

  1. double it
  2. add 3 additional inches



This gives you the approximate length needed for each strand of embroidery floss.

For example, if your wrist measures 6 inches:

  * 6 × 2 = 12 inches
  * 12 + 3 = 15 inches



You would cut each piece of embroidery floss to approximately 15 inches.

This extra length gives you room to make the knots and tie the finished bracelet.

## Step 2: Cut the Embroidery Floss

 

![Measuring and cutting three colors of embroidery floss for a spiral friendship bracelet](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/spiral-bracelet-step-2-measure-embroidery-floss.webp)

 

Cut one strand in each of your chosen colors.

If you are making the bracelet with three colors, you will have three strands.

Try to make all strands approximately the same length.

Keep them together before moving to the next step.

## Step 3: Tie the Starting Knot

![Three embroidery floss strands tied together with a starting knot for a spiral friendship bracelet](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/spiral-bracelet-step-3-starting-knot.webp)

 

Gather all of your embroidery floss strands together.

Tie a knot near one end.

Leave approximately **2 inches of loose floss above the knot**.

Do not trim this section.

You will use the extra floss later to help tie the bracelet around your wrist.

## Step 4: Secure the Bracelet to Your Work Surface

Place the bracelet on a flat surface.

Use a piece of tape to secure the loose end above the starting knot.

The strands should hang downward toward you.

Keeping the bracelet secured makes it much easier to control the thread while making the knots.

Make sure the tape is firm enough that the floss does not move every time you tighten a knot.

# Making the First Spiral Color Section

Choose which color you want to appear first.

In the example pattern, the first color is purple.

## Step 5: Separate the First Color

Take the first embroidery floss color and move it away from the other strands.

The remaining colors should stay together in one group.

The separated strand becomes your working thread.

## Step 6: Cross the Working Floss Over the Other Strands

Take your selected working strand and bring it **over the grouped strands**.

This should create a loop beside the other floss.

Keep the remaining colors together while you work.

## Step 7: Pass the Working Floss Underneath

Bring the working floss underneath the grouped strands.

Then pull it back through the loop on the same side where the working thread originally started.

You should now have a loose knot wrapped around the other colors.

## Step 8: Tighten the Knot

 

![](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/spiral-bracelet-step-4-first-color-knot.webp)

 

Pull the working strand upward until the knot slides toward the top of the bracelet.

Tighten it neatly against the previous knot.

Do not pull so aggressively that the other embroidery floss becomes distorted.

The knot should feel secure but still look smooth.

## Step 9: Repeat the Knot

Continue using the same color.

Repeat the same knot **7 to 15 times** , depending on how long you want each color section to be.

The more knots you make, the larger the section of that color will appear.

As you continue, you should notice the bracelet beginning to **spiral naturally**.

You do not need to manually twist the bracelet.

The repeated knotting technique creates the spiral on its own.

# Adding the Second Color

Once the first color section is the size you want, switch to your second color.

In the example, the second color is yellow.

## Step 10: Separate the Second Color

Return the first working color to the group.

Now separate the second color.

This second color becomes your new working strand.

The other strands remain together in the center.

## Step 11: Make the Same Knot

 

![Tying the yellow embroidery floss around the other strands for the second spiral bracelet color](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/spiral-bracelet-step-5-second-color-knot.webp)

 

Cross the second color over the other strands.

Pass it underneath.

Pull it through the loop on the same side.

Then slide the knot upward and tighten.

Repeat the same motion.

Try to make approximately the **same number of knots** that you used for the first color section.

For example, if you made 10 purple knots, make approximately 10 yellow knots.

This helps the color sections look balanced.

# Adding the Third Color

Now switch to your third embroidery floss color.

In the example, the third color is pink.

## Step 12: Separate the Third Color

Place the second color back with the other strands.

Pull out your third color.

Keep the remaining threads together.

## Step 13: Repeat the Spiral Knot

 

![Tying the coral pink embroidery floss to create the third color section of the spiral bracelet](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/spiral-bracelet-step-6-third-color-knot.webp)

 

Use exactly the same technique:

  1. Cross the working thread over the grouped strands.
  2. Pass it underneath.
  3. Pull it through the loop.
  4. Tighten the knot at the top.
  5. Repeat.



Again, make roughly the same number of knots used for the previous colors.

After completing the third section, you should have three colorful sections wrapping around your bracelet.

# Step 14: Repeat the Color Sequence

Return to your first color and begin again.

For example:

**Purple → Yellow → Pink → Purple → Yellow → Pink**

Continue repeating the colors in the same order.

You can also create your own sequence.

For example:

**Blue → White → Blue → Pink**

or:

**Red → Orange → Yellow → Green → Blue**

The method remains the same regardless of the color combination.

# Step 15: Check the Bracelet Length

 

![Nearly finished spiral friendship bracelet with loose embroidery floss ends ready for finishing](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/spiral-bracelet-step-7-check-length.webp)

 

After repeating the color sequence several times, compare the bracelet to your wrist.

Wrap the bracelet gently around the wrist without pulling it too tightly.

If the bracelet is still too short, continue adding more color sections.

Remember to leave enough loose floss at the end for tying the bracelet.

# Step 16: Finish the Bracelet

When the bracelet reaches the length you want, gather all the floss strands together.

Tie a firm knot at the end, similar to the knot you made when you started.

You should now have loose floss at both ends of the bracelet.

Use those ends to tie the bracelet around your wrist.

Your spiral friendship bracelet is now finished.

 

![Finished colorful spiral friendship bracelet made with purple, coral pink, and yellow embroidery floss](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/finished-spiral-friendship-bracelet.webp)

 

# How to Keep the Spiral Looking Even

## Use the Same Number of Knots for Each Color

If you want each color section to be approximately the same size, count your knots.

For example:

  * Purple: 10 knots
  * Yellow: 10 knots
  * Pink: 10 knots



Then repeat.

You can also intentionally make some sections longer for a different look.

## Keep Consistent Tension

Uneven tension can cause some sections to look wider or tighter than others.

Try to pull each knot with approximately the same amount of pressure.

## Keep the Bracelet Secure

If the taped end keeps moving, your knots may become difficult to control.

Replace the tape or use a clipboard if necessary.

## Let the Bracelet Twist Naturally

Do not worry if the pattern begins turning as you knot.

That twisting is exactly what creates the spiral design.

# How Many Knots Should You Make Per Color?

The original method suggests approximately **7 to 15 knots per color section**.

There is no single correct number.

Use fewer knots if you want:

  * shorter color blocks
  * frequent color changes
  * a more mixed appearance



Use more knots if you want:

  * larger sections of each color
  * bold color blocks
  * fewer color transitions



A good beginner starting point is around **10 knots per color**.

# Two-Color Spiral Bracelet Variation

You do not need three colors.

For a simple two-color version:

  1. choose two colors
  2. make 7 to 15 knots using the first color
  3. switch to the second color
  4. make the same number of knots
  5. repeat



For example:

**Black → White → Black → White**

This creates a simple repeating spiral.

# Five-Color Spiral Bracelet Variation

For a brighter friendship bracelet, use five embroidery floss colors.

For example:

**Pink → Orange → Yellow → Green → Blue**

Work through each color before returning to the first one.

Because there are more colors, you may want to use slightly shorter sections so that all five colors appear several times before the bracelet is finished.

# Spiral Friendship Bracelet Troubleshooting

## My Bracelet Is Not Spiraling

Make sure you are repeating the same knot in the same direction while working each color section.

The repeating knot naturally causes the bracelet to rotate.

If you alternate knot directions, the bracelet may become flatter instead.

## My Spiral Looks Loose

Try tightening each knot a little more before beginning the next one.

Also make sure the starting end is securely taped to your work surface.

## My Color Sections Are Different Sizes

Count your knots.

If one section has 8 knots and another has 15, the color blocks will be noticeably different.

That is not necessarily wrong, but counting helps if you want a more uniform pattern.

## My Embroidery Floss Is Getting Tangled

Separate the strands frequently.

Let the floss hang naturally and untwist it whenever necessary.

Working on a clean flat surface also helps.

## One Strand Is Becoming Too Short

The working strands use more floss than strands that spend more time grouped in the middle.

If you plan to make especially long color sections, start with extra embroidery floss.

Yes. This bracelet is beginner-friendly because it uses one simple repeating knot.

Once you understand how to complete the first section, you repeat the same technique with every color.

You can use as many colors as you like, but **2 to 5 colors** is a practical range for this project.

Three colors are especially easy for beginners.

The spiral forms because the same type of knot is repeatedly tied in the same direction.

As more knots are added, the thread naturally rotates around the center strands.

Make approximately **7 to 15 knots per color**.

Choose fewer knots for small color sections and more knots for larger blocks.

## Can I use embroidery floss for a spiral bracelet?

Yes. Embroidery floss works well for this project and makes it easy to create colorful friendship bracelets.

# Easy Spiral Friendship Bracelet Pattern Summary

For a quick reference, the basic process is:

  1. Measure your wrist.
  2. Double the measurement and add 3 inches.
  3. Cut your embroidery floss.
  4. Tie the strands together.
  5. Leave about 2 inches at the top.
  6. Tape the bracelet to a flat surface.
  7. Separate your first color.
  8. Knot it around the remaining strands 7 to 15 times.
  9. Switch to the second color.
  10. Repeat with every color.
  11. Continue until the bracelet fits your wrist.
  12. Tie a knot at the end.
  13. Tie the two ends together to wear the bracelet.
