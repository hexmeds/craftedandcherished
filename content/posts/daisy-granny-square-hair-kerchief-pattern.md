Some makes just feel like summer, and this kerchief is exactly that kind of project. Soft cream granny squares — some blooming with puffy little daisies in yellow and white — join together into a triangle that drapes over the hair like something straight out of a countryside picnic. A sage-green scalloped border frames the whole piece, and ties at the corners keep everything snug through a breezy afternoon. Worn over loose waves or braids, it's cottagecore at its prettiest.

If you love the idea of a triangle head accessory but want to try a different style next, take a look at our [modern floral crochet bandana](https://craftedandcherished.com/modern-crochet-bandana-pattern-that-feels-fresh-minimal/) or the [boho lace floral crochet bandana](https://craftedandcherished.com/boho-lace-floral-crochet-bandana-pattern/) for two very different takes on the same wearable shape.

What makes this kerchief especially satisfying to crochet is that it's really a collection of small, quick wins rather than one long project. Each daisy square comes together as its own little treat — a puffy petal flower that grows outward into a granny square — and once you've made a handful, joining them and adding the border goes fast. It's portable, uses up scraps beautifully, and is the kind of thing you can finish over a weekend without it ever feeling like a chore.

**Difficulty level:** friendly intermediate. If you've made a granny square before, the only new skill here is the puff-stitch daisy center, and it's much easier than it looks. Gauge is forgiving too, since a kerchief drapes rather than fits closely — making this a low-pressure way to practice motifs and joining techniques.

## Materials Needed

**Yarn:** DK or light worsted (#3) cotton or cotton-blend yarn

  * Cream: approximately 150–200 yards (for the squares and joining)
  * White: a small amount for the daisy petals (under 25 yards)
  * Soft yellow: scraps for the daisy centers
  * Sage green: approximately 40–60 yards (for the border, scallops, and ties)



**Hook:** US G/6 (4.0mm)

**Notions:**

  * Yarn needle for joining and weaving in ends
  * Scissors
  * Stitch markers (helpful while joining squares)
  * Rust-proof pins for blocking
  * Measuring tape



## Abbreviations Used (US Terms)

  * ch — chain
  * sc — single crochet
  * dc — double crochet
  * sl st — slip stitch
  * st(s) — stitch(es)
  * sp — space
  * MR — magic ring
  * puff — puff stitch: (yarn over, insert hook, pull up a loop) 3–4 times into the same stitch or space, yarn over and pull through all the loops on your hook, then chain 1 to close
  * rep — repeat



## Pattern Notes

This pattern uses US crochet terminology throughout.

The kerchief is built from individual granny square motifs — a mix of daisy squares and plain squares — each worked in the round, then joined together into a triangle layout and finished off with a green scallop border and corner ties.

Gauge here is fairly relaxed: each finished square should measure about 3 inches (7.5cm). Getting the exact gauge matters less than staying consistent — squares that are all the same size will join up cleanly no matter the precise measurement. At 3-inch squares, the layout below produces a kerchief around 15 inches (38cm) across the front edge, which fits most adults comfortably.

**Layout:** the squares form a triangle — a row of 4 across the front edge, then 3, then 2, then 1 at the back point, for 10 squares total. You can mix the daisy and plain squares however you like; using 6 daisy squares and 4 plain squares, scattered throughout, matches the look shown in the photos.

Stitch counts appear in parentheses at the end of each round.

**Finished size:** approximately 15 inches (38cm) along the front edge and 11 inches (28cm) from front to back point, plus the ties. See the FAQ section below for resizing notes.

## Pattern Instructions

### The Daisy Square (make 6)

**Round 1 (center):** With yellow yarn, make a magic ring, ch 1, work 8 sc into the ring, pull it closed, and join with a sl st to the first sc. _(8 sts)_ Fasten off the yellow.

**Round 2 (petals):** Join white yarn in any stitch. Work [a puff stitch into the next stitch, ch 1] eight times around, then join to the top of the first puff. _(8 puff petals)_ Pull each puff stitch's loops up generously so the petals sit plump and rounded, just like a real daisy. Fasten off the white.

**Round 3 (squaring it up):** Join cream yarn in any ch-1 space between the petals. Ch 3 (counts as your first dc), work 2 more dc into the same space; in the next space work 3 dc; in the following space work (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) to form a corner. Repeat this sequence around so that four evenly spaced ch-1 spaces become your corners, with plain 3-dc groups filling the spaces between them, then join.

**Round 4:** Slip stitch over to the first corner space. Ch 3, then work (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) into that corner space. Work 3 dc into each side space along the edge, and (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) into each corner space as you go. Repeat all the way around and join. This completes one daisy square, approximately 3 inches across.

Fasten off, leaving a tail for joining later.

### The Plain Square (make 4)

**Round 1:** With cream yarn, make a magic ring, ch 3 (counts as dc), work 2 more dc into the ring, ch 2, then 3 dc, ch 2 — repeat this two more times, then join. _(4 corner groups)_

**Rounds 2–3:** Slip stitch to the corner space, ch 3, work (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) into the corner; 3 dc into each side space; (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) into each corner. Repeat around, joining at the end of each round. This produces a square matching the size of the daisy squares, approximately 3 inches across.

If your plain squares end up smaller than your daisy squares, add a round of sc around the plain ones until the sizes match — consistent sizing matters more than matching round counts.

Fasten off, leaving tails for joining.

### Joining Into the Kerchief Triangle

Lay your squares out in the triangle formation: a front row of 4 squares, then 3, then 2, then 1 at the back point, scattering the daisy squares among the plain ones however looks best to you.

To join, use cream yarn and a yarn needle to whip stitch through the back loops, or use a sl-st or sc join instead. Seam the squares row by row first, then join the rows together, keeping your tension consistent throughout so the finished piece drapes evenly. Don't worry about the stepped, diagonal edges along the sides of the triangle at this stage — the border will smooth those out.

### The Border & Scallops (Sage Green)

**Round 1 (smoothing round):** Join green yarn at a front corner. Work sc evenly all the way around the triangle — along the front edge and both diagonal edges — placing enough stitches across each square and 3 sc at the outer corners to keep the piece lying flat. Along the stepped diagonal edges, ease your stitch placement so the steps round out into a smooth line.

**Round 2 (scallops):** Sc in the next stitch, skip 2 stitches, work 5 dc into the next stitch, skip 2 stitches — repeat this around both diagonal edges. Along the straight front edge, you can either continue the same scallop repeat or switch to plain sc for a flatter edge near the hairline; working scallops all the way around matches the look in the original photos, so continue the repeat there too if you'd like the same finish. Join and fasten off.

### The Ties

At each end of the front edge, join green yarn and work a chain of 40–50 stitches for a simple tie. For something sturdier, you can instead work a slip-stitch cord or a narrow 3-stitch sc strip about 10–12 inches long. Fasten off and weave in the ends. The ties knot together beneath the hair at the nape of the neck.

## Tips & Tricks

  * **Make all your squares first, then compare them.** Stack the finished squares together — any that are off in size will show up immediately, and it's much easier to add an equalizing round now than to fight an uneven join later.
  * **Plump up your puff stitches.** Pull each loop of the puff stitch up tall — about half an inch — before closing it. Skimpy loops make flat petals, while generous loops make the daisies look full and rounded.
  * **Block your squares before joining.** Pin each one out to 3 inches and let it set. Blocked motifs join together like neat tiles instead of fighting each other into odd shapes.
  * **Join with matching tension.** A joining row that's tighter than the squares themselves will pucker the whole kerchief. Keep your joins loose and even to preserve a soft drape over the head.
  * **Ease the diagonal edges as you border.** The stepped edge of a squared-off triangle usually needs slightly fewer border stitches than you'd expect — if your border starts to ruffle, pull it back and space your sc round a bit tighter.
  * **Try it on before adding the ties.** Drape the bordered triangle over your head and check the front-edge length. If it feels too narrow or too wide, adjust the tie length to compensate, or add an extra round of sc along the front edge.

## Finishing

Weave in all your ends — this motif-based project generates plenty of tails, so bury each one in two directions through matching-color stitches and trim close.

Wet-block or steam-block the finished kerchief flat, pinning the scallops open so each little scallop sets crisply into shape.

Attach or finish off the ties, knot the ends, and trim.

To wear: drape it over the crown of your head with the point sitting at the back, tie it beneath your hair at the nape, and pull a few loose strands free for that easy, effortless look.

## FAQ

**How do I resize it for a child or a larger head?** You have two main levers: square size (a smaller hook and finer yarn will shrink every square) and layout (a smaller 3-2-1 triangle made from six squares gives a sweet child's size, while adding a fifth square to the front row and a fourth row overall creates a more generous adult drape). Check the front-edge length against an ear-to-ear-over-the-crown measurement, plus a little extra ease.

**My daisy squares and plain squares came out different sizes — help?** That's completely normal, since puff-stitch rounds tend to work up denser than plain granny rounds. Add an equalizing round of sc (or another granny round) to whichever squares are smaller until the sizes match. Matching size matters more than matching round count.

**The border is ruffling and the kerchief won 't lie flat — what went wrong?** This is almost always caused by too many stitches in the smoothing round along the diagonal edges. Unravel the border, rework the sc round with slightly fewer, more evenly spaced stitches, and block again — it should lie flat quickly after that.

**Can I make it in other colors?** Absolutely. Keep the daisies white and yellow and swap the cream base for sage, dusty blue, or lavender, or go for a full meadow look with mixed pastel squares. A neutral border ties any color combination together nicely.
