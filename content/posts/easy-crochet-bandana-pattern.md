There is nothing quite like a quick, stylish crochet project to kick off the spring and summer seasons! This easy crochet bandana is incredibly versatile—you can make one for yourself, gift it to a friend, or even size it down to create an adorable crochet dog bandana pattern. It is the perfect grab-and-go project to toss in your bag for road trips or lazy evenings catching up on your favorite shows.

Using a lightweight cotton yarn, this bandana scarf is wonderfully cool and breathable for warmer weather. Whether you are a beginner looking for a fun introduction to shell stitches or an experienced crocheter wanting a relaxing, stash-busting make, this pattern is sure to become a favorite. Watch the full [video tutorial here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=49FBB2IPHBk) to see exactly how it comes together!

_This pattern uses USA crochet terminology._

## Materials Needed

  * 1 skein of cotton worsted weight yarn
  * 5.00 mm crochet hook
  * Large eye needle
  * Scissors



## Abbreviations Used

  * **Ch** = Chain
  * **Sc** = Single Crochet
  * **Dc** = Double Crochet
  * **Sl st** = Slip Stitch
  * **St** = Stitch



## Pattern Instructions

### Special Stitches

**Magic loop:** wrap yarn around hand, insert hook into loop and pull up a loop. Remove your hand from the loop, chain 3 and begin pattern.

**Shell stitch:** 5 dc in space, sl st in center dc of next granny cluster.

### Gauge & Size

  * **Gauge:** 4 granny clusters = 4″.
  * **Finished Size:** approx. 18″ across (not counting ties) and approx. 10″ long



### Notes

  * Chain 3 counts as first double crochet in every row



### Bandana Body

**Row 1:** Make a magic loop. Ch 3 (counts as first st), then (2 dc, ch 1, 3 dc) in loop.

Pull short end of yarn to tighten. Turn.

**Row 2:** Ch 3, 2 dc into same st, (3 dc, ch 1, 3 dc) in corner space, 3 dc in top of ch 3. Turn.

**Row 3:** Ch 3, 2 dc into same st. 3 dc in next space. (3 dc, ch 1, 3 dc) in corner space. 3 dc in next space. 3 dc in top of ch 3. Turn.

**Row 4 – 13:** Ch 3, 2 dc into same st. 3 dc in all spaces along first side. (3 dc, ch 1, 3 dc) in corner ch space. 3 dc in all spaces along this side. 3 dc in top of ch 3. Turn.

Leave yarn attached for next part.

### Scalloped Edge

Ch 1, sk first dc, sl st into next dc.

Make 5 dc in space. Sl st into center dc of 3 dc cluster. Repeat to corner.

Make 7 dc into corner space, then continue 5 dc scallop pattern down the other side.

Last sl st will be in st right before ch 3 of previous row. Leave yarn attached for next part.

### Ties

Ch 61. Sk first ch, sl st into 2nd ch from hook and each sl st down the row.

Sc in same st that tie is attached to. Sc evenly down the straight side of bandana until you reach the other side.

I made 55 sc, but yours may vary. _Note: If you sc over the yarn end from the magic loop, you won’t have to weave it in later!_

Ch 61. Sk first ch, sl st into 2nd ch from hook and each sl st down the row.

Sl st into side of st at base of tie.

Finish off. Weave in ends and trim off excess. Done!

## Tips

  * **Yarn Selection:** The sample uses a worsted weight cotton yarn ("I Love This Cotton" in "Rosey" from Hobby Lobby), which works beautifully. Cotton is highly recommended for this pattern because it is cool, breathable, and perfect for warm spring and summer weather.
  * **Stash Buster:** You will only need one skein of yarn for this project—and you likely won't even use the whole ball! It is a fantastic way to use up random leftover yarn from your stash.
  * **Yarn Substitution Warning:** You can use any worsted weight yarn you like, but keep in mind that acrylic yarn stretches out significantly more than cotton yarn will over time.

## FAQ

**Can I make this as a dog bandana?** Absolutely! This is a highly versatile pattern. You can easily adjust the starting chain or stop crocheting earlier to create the perfect size for your furry friend. If you're making pet accessories, you might also love this [Daisy Granny Square Hair Kerchief Pattern](https://craftedandcherished.com/daisy-granny-square-hair-kerchief-pattern/).

**How do I adjust the size?** To make it smaller or larger, simply add or subtract rows before working the scalloped edge. Just remember that changing the size will alter how much yarn you need for the foundation and ties.

**What if my scallops look uneven?** Count your stitches carefully as you go. Making sure you have exactly 3 double crochets in each space along the sides, and 7 in the corners, will keep your edges perfectly uniform.
