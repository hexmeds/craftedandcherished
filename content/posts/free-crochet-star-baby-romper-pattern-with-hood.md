Looking for a sweet and cozy crochet project for a little one? This **free crochet star baby romper pattern** is a fun handmade project featuring a beautiful star design, a comfortable hood, side button closures, and a soft fit for babies.

The romper is designed for approximately **0–3 months** , but the construction can be adjusted for larger sizes by adding additional increase rows. It can be made with your favorite yarn colors, making it a lovely handmade baby shower gift or a cozy addition to a baby's wardrobe.

The star design gives this baby romper a unique look, while the hood and button opening make it practical and easy to put on.

## About This Crochet Baby Romper

This crochet baby romper is constructed around a five-point star motif. The upper section is worked with increases at five points, creating the star shape. Individual star sections are then shaped with decreases before the front and back are assembled.

The finished romper includes:

  * A five-star back section
  * A three-star front section
  * Separate sleeves
  * A wide hood
  * Three buttonholes
  * Three buttons
  * Single crochet edging
  * Reverse single crochet around the collar



The original tutorial makes the sample for a **0–3 month baby** and measures approximately **28 cm wide** at the increase section.

# How to Crochet the Star Baby Romper

## Step 1: Begin the Star Motif

Start with a magic ring.

### Round 1

![](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/1ch.webp)

 

Make a magic ring.

Ch 3. The beginning chain counts as the first dc.

Work 2 dc into the magic ring.

Ch 1.

Work 3 dc into the ring.

Ch 1.

Repeat the following sequence until you have five groups:

**3 dc, ch 1**

You should have **5 groups of 3 dc** , separated by chain-1 spaces.

Pull the magic ring closed.

Join to the top of the beginning ch-3 with a sl st.

You now have five sections around the center of the star.

 

![](https://craftedandcherished.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/3-sc.webp)

# Step 2: Work the Increase Section

The star grows by adding stitches at each of the five chain spaces.

At each increase point, work:

**1 dc, ch 1, 1 dc**

This increase is repeated at all five points.

At the beginning of each row, use the turning chain specified in the tutorial and turn the work.

Continue working across each section and make an increase at every chain-space increase point.

As the rows progress, the star becomes wider.

For the sample size, continue until you have completed **14 increase rows**.

The final width of the sample is approximately **28 cm**.

### Making a Larger Size

If you want a larger romper, continue the increase pattern for additional rows.

The original tutorial suggests approximately:

  * One extra increase row for 3–6 months
  * Two extra increase rows for 6–12 months



Because babies vary considerably in size, use the baby's measurements whenever possible rather than relying only on age ranges.

# Step 3: Separate the Five Star Sections

Once the increase section is complete, begin working the individual star points.

The tutorial works the sections using **31 stitches at the widest point**.

For each star section, work three rows without decreases.

### Star Row 1

Ch 3 and turn.

Work 1 dc in each stitch across.

**31 dc**

### Star Row 2

Ch 1 and turn.

Work 1 dc in each stitch across.

**31 dc**

### Star Row 3

Ch 1 and turn.

Work 1 dc in each stitch across.

**31 dc**

At this point, the star section is ready for shaping.

# Step 4: Shape the Star Point

Beginning with the next row, decrease at both ends of every row.

### Decrease Row

Ch 1 and turn.

Skip the first stitch.

Work 1 dc into the second stitch.

Continue working dc across until 2 stitches remain.

Work the final 2 stitches together as a dc decrease.

You have now decreased by 2 stitches.

The stitch counts progress like this:

Row | Stitch Count  
---|---  
Starting row | 31  
Decrease 1 | 29  
Decrease 2 | 27  
Decrease 3 | 25  
Decrease 4 | 23  
Continue | Decrease by 2 each row  
  
Continue decreasing at both ends until only **3 stitches remain**.

This creates the pointed end of the star.

# Step 5: Complete the Five Stars for the Back

Repeat the same star-point construction to create the five sections needed for the back.

Each star is worked to the same height.

The tutorial makes:

  * 5 star sections
  * 3 straight rows at the widest part
  * Decreases at both ends
  * A pointed top ending with 3 stitches



Keeping each star the same height is important so the finished back joins neatly.

# Step 6: Add a Single Crochet Edge to the Back

Work single crochet around the outer edge of the completed five-star back.

Along the long shaped edges, work approximately **2 sc per row**.

At the corners and star points, add stitches as needed to keep the edge smooth and flat.

The original tutorial emphasizes keeping the stitch count consistent on corresponding sides of the piece.

When you reach the beginning:

Join with a sl st.

Fasten off.

# Step 7: Crochet the Front

The front is constructed using the same star technique.

For the front, the tutorial makes **three completed star sections** and leaves the remaining area open for the front opening and hood.

Work the star sections using the same increase and decrease construction as the back.

For the sample, the front is worked to approximately the same height before the opening is formed.

# Step 8: Add the Buttonholes

The front opening is finished with a buttonhole band.

Attach your yarn at the designated increase chain.

Ch 3.

Work dc stitches across the edge.

The tutorial uses **three buttonholes**.

### To Make Each Buttonhole

At the marked location:

  1. Ch 1.
  2. Skip 1 stitch.
  3. Work 1 dc in the next stitch.
  4. Continue working dc across.



Repeat this process at the remaining marked locations.

Make sure the buttonholes are large enough for the buttons you selected.

The original tutorial positions three buttonholes along the front edge.

# Step 9: Finish the Front Edge

After completing the buttonholes, work a row of hdc along the front edge.

Ch 1 and turn.

Work 1 hdc into each stitch and chain space across.

Fasten off.

Then add the same neat single crochet edging around the star sections as you did on the back.

# Step 10: Crochet the Sleeves

The sleeves are made separately and then attached to the body.

For each sleeve:

**Ch 34**

Skip the first 3 chains and work a dc into the fourth chain from the hook.

Continue working dc across.

You should have:

**31 dc**

The original tutorial uses four rows of 31 dc before beginning the sleeve decreases.

### Sleeve Rows 2–4

Ch 1 and turn.

Work 1 dc in each stitch across.

Repeat until you have completed four rows.

# Step 11: Shape the Sleeve

Beginning with the next row, decrease at both ends.

Ch 1 and turn.

Skip the first stitch.

Dc in the second stitch.

Continue working dc until 2 stitches remain.

Work the final 2 stitches together.

Repeat this decrease on every row.

The sleeve section is approximately **18 rows total** in the sample, including the four initial rows.

# Step 12: Edge the Sleeve

Work sc around the entire sleeve section.

As with the body edging, work approximately **2 sc per row** along the shaped edges.

Keep the edging consistent so the sleeve can be joined smoothly to the body.

Fasten off.

Repeat for the second sleeve.

# Step 13: Crochet the Hood

The hood begins with a larger foundation chain than the sleeves.

This gives the hood enough room for a baby's head.

### Foundation Chain

**Ch 62**

The tutorial uses 62 chains for the hood.

### Hood Row 1

Dc in the fourth chain from the hook.

Dc across.

You should have:

**59 dc**

# Step 14: Add Texture to the Hood

The next row uses alternating front- and back-positioned stitches to create a knit-and-purl-like texture.

Ch 1 and turn.

Work the alternating textured dc stitches across the row.

The tutorial uses this textured row to give the hood a slightly ribbed appearance.

For babies under one year, the tutorial suggests that one textured row is sufficient. For an older child, additional textured rows can be considered.

# Step 15: Shape the Hood

Work one row of treble crochet across the hood.

You should have:

**59 tr**

From the following row onward, begin decreasing.

Unlike the star sections, the hood decreases by **two stitches at each side of the row**.

That means four stitches are removed from the total stitch count per decrease row.

Continue the decreases until the hood reaches the desired shape.

The original tutorial works the hood for approximately **17 rows**.

# Step 16: Edge the Hood

Work single crochet around the outside edge of the hood.

Along the shaped rows, use approximately:

**2 sc per row**

Continue around the hood.

Do not fasten off if you want to use the attached yarn directly for joining.

# Step 17: Join the Hood

Place the hood pieces together with the correct sides positioned as shown in the tutorial.

Join the corresponding stitches along the hood seam.

The original tutorial uses a crochet joining technique in which the hook passes through corresponding stitches on both sides.

Pull the yarn through both stitches to create the seam.

If you prefer a simpler method, you can join the hood with single crochet stitches.

# Step 18: Join the Front and Back

Place the front and back sections together.

Make sure the right sides are facing correctly.

Match the star sections.

Join the corresponding stitches along the body.

Continue until you reach the armhole opening.

The tutorial uses the same stitch-to-stitch joining method used for the hood.

# Step 19: Attach the Sleeves

Position the sleeve inside the armhole opening.

Make sure the right side of the sleeve is facing outward.

Match the stitches carefully.

The tutorial leaves a small section unjoined at the beginning and end of the armhole to accommodate the construction.

Join the sleeve around the armhole, matching the stitches one by one.

Continue until the sleeve is securely attached.

Repeat for the second sleeve.

# Step 20: Sew on the Buttons

Once the romper is assembled, sew the three buttons opposite the buttonholes.

Before sewing them permanently, close the buttonholes and check that the placement is correct.

The original tutorial uses three buttons but notes that two buttons can also be used if preferred.

Make sure every button is securely attached.

# Step 21: Finish the Collar

For the final neckline finish, work reverse single crochet around the collar.

Attach the yarn to the collar edge.

Work reverse single crochet around the entire neckline.

This creates a neat decorative border and gives the collar a finished appearance.

Join with a sl st.

Fasten off and weave in the ends.

The tutorial uses reverse single crochet around the collar as its final edging.

# Tips for Making the Crochet Star Baby Romper

## Choose a soft yarn

Since this romper is intended for a baby, choose a yarn that feels comfortable against delicate skin.

A soft baby yarn is a good choice, while velvet yarn can create an especially plush result.

## Don't crochet too tightly

The original sample was intentionally crocheted with a relatively relaxed tension so the finished fabric would remain soft.

If your stitches are very tight, try going up to a 3.5 or 4 mm hook.

## Keep your star sections consistent

When making multiple stars, count your rows carefully.

All five back stars should have the same height, and the front sections should be worked consistently.

## Check the fit as you go

Age-based sizing is only an estimate.

If you are making the romper for a particular baby, compare the project against the baby's actual measurements whenever possible.

## Mark the buttonholes

Use stitch markers to mark your buttonhole positions before beginning the buttonhole row.

This makes it much easier to keep the three openings evenly spaced.
