Looking for a quick and rewarding crochet project? This simple crochet scarf pattern with tassels is perfect for beginners and experienced crafters alike. Using just two basic stitches, you'll create a beautifully textured scarf that looks elegant and modern, yet is incredibly easy to make.

This beginner-friendly pattern works up quickly, making it an ideal project for cozy evenings or weekend crafting sessions. The finished scarf is stylish enough for everyday wear and makes a wonderful handmade gift for someone special.

## Materials Needed

  * Yarn: Approximately 400 yards/365 meters of worsted weight (4) acrylic yarn (I used Lion Brand Pound of Love in Thistle)
  * Crochet Hook: K/10.5 (6.50 mm) or size needed to obtain gauge
  * Yarn needle
  * Scissors
  * Measuring tape
  * Optional: A piece of cardboard or object 4″ wide (to help cut tassels evenly)



## Abbreviations Used

  * ch = chain
  * st = stitch
  * sts = stitches
  * sl st = slip stitch
  * sc = single crochet
  * dc = double crochet
  * RS = right side
  * WS = wrong side



_This pattern uses USA crochet terminology_

## Pattern Instructions

### Scarf

_If you crochet tightly, you may want to go up a hook size for the starting chains (only) to help you work more easily into the back ridge loops on Row 1._

Start: Chain 21.

For Row 1 (only) work into the back ridge loops.

**Row 1 (RS):** In the 2nd chain from hook, sc. In the next chain, dc. _In the next chain, sc. In the next chain, dc._ Repeat from * to * across entire row (20 sts). Ch 1 and turn.

**Row 2 (WS):** Sc into the 1st st (the top of the last dc you completed). Dc into the next stitch (the top of the sc from the previous row). _Sc into the next st. Dc into the next stitch._ Repeat from * to * across entire row (20 sts). Ch 1 and turn.

_After Row 2, scarf width should measure approx 7 ″._

**Row 3 and on:** Repeat Row 2 until your scarf height is 6 feet (approximately 168 rows) or your desired height. _(I recommend ending after an even number / wrong side row)._

Fasten off and weave in loose ends.

### Adding Tassels

You will need 60 x 8″ strands of yarn for the tassels (for tassels that are approx 3.75″ long). To make the tassels shorter or longer, cut smaller or bigger strands.

_Tip for cutting the strands: find an object that is 4 ″ wide (a piece of cardboard, a notebook, etc). Wrap your yarn around the 4″ object 20 times. Cut the yarn in half. Repeat two more times (until you have 60 strands)._

#### Tassel Placement

Ensure the right side (RS) of your scarf is facing you. At the start of your scarf (the chain/foundation row), put tassels through the chain spaces. At the end (after your last completed row), put tassels through the tops of the stitches.

I recommend putting tassels in the first ch/st, skipping two chains/sts and then placing tassels in every other ch/st across (10 tassels in total).

  1. Take three strands of yarn, fold them in half and either with a hook or your fingers, pull the folded ends through the stitch (which creates a loop).
  2. Pull the ends through the loop.
  3. Pull tight to make a knot.



Repeat for each tassel placement.

Trim the strands to make them all one length (I cut mine to be approx. 3.75″).

## Tips

  * To adjust the width of your scarf, change your starting chain in multiples of 2 + 1 (meaning your number of starting chains will be an odd number).
  * To adjust the length, simply work fewer or more rows until you reach your desired height.
  * The pattern creates a scarf that is 7″ wide and 6′ long as written.
  * For consistent gauge, 4″ x 4″ = 13 sts and 10 rows following the pattern.
  * If your starting tail is on the left, it's a right side (RS) row. If the starting tail is on the right, it's a wrong side (WS) row.
