Wrap yourself in ultimate cozy comfort with this stunning Color Block Super Scarf! Unlike standard scarves, this "super" version is designed with extra width and length, allowing you to wrap it multiple ways while staying incredibly warm during cold winter months. The gorgeous texture created by the blanket stitch gives this scarf a squishy, luxurious feel that's perfectly gender-neutral and versatile for any style.

This pattern is ideal for advanced beginners who are comfortable with basic stitches and reading patterns. The color blocking adds visual interest and makes this scarf a perfect project to show off team spirit, school colors, or your personal color preferences. ## Materials Needed

  * Medium (4) weight acrylic yarn in three colors: 
    * Color A: 124 grams / 230 yards / 4.4 ounces
    * Color B: 57 grams / 101 yards / 2 ounces
    * Color C: 280 grams / 460 yards / 10 ounces
  * 5 mm (H-8) hook
  * Tapestry needle
  * Scissors
  * Stitch Markers



_Note: The sample uses I Love This Yarn in Dark Denim (Color A), Sungold (Color B) and Ivory (Color C). This yarn is 100% Acrylic – 7 ounces / 355 yards per skein._

For more cozy accessories like this one, explore our [cozy crochet accessories patterns](https://craftedandcherished.com/cozy-crochet-accessories-patterns-warm-stylish/) collection.

## Abbreviations Used

  * Ch(s) – Chain(s)
  * Sc- Single crochet
  * Dc- Double crochet
  * Sk – Skip
  * St(s) – Stitch(es)
  * (…) – Instructions inside parentheses indicate to work all stitches in one stitch.
  * […] – Repeats are listed inside brackets
  * (…) – Stitch counts are listed at end of row in ( ) and bold italics.



This pattern uses USA crochet terminology.

## Pattern Notes

  * This scarf is designed to be oversized. There are instructions on how to change the size, if you'd like to alter it.
  * Chain 2 at the start of the row does not count as a stitch in the stitch count. You will always place your first stitch(es) in the very first stitch of the row.
  * At the end of each row, you will work into (or around) the chain 2 turning chain (even though it doesn't count as a stitch).
  * At each color change, cut your yarn and tie it off. You can weave in the ends immediately or wait until the end.
  * You can change the yarn weight or yarn fiber if you prefer. Keep in mind, if you use a different weight or fiber, your yarn usage will be different than listed.
  * This pattern requires a multiple of 3 chains. Any multiple of 3 chains will work with this stitch pattern.



### Finished Size

  * Length: Approximately 90 inches (with fringe).
  * Width: Approximately 9 inches.



### Gauge

19 stitches x 11 rows = 4 inches x 4 inches.

Pattern for gauge: Chain 21. Follow pattern instructions through Row 11.

## Pattern Instructions

With Color A, Ch 39.

**Row 1:** – In 3rd ch from hook, place 2 dc. [Sk 2 chs, place (1 sc, 2 dc) in next ch]. Repeat inside brackets until 3 chs remain. Sk 2 ch, place 1 sc in last ch. _(36)_

**Row 2:** – Ch 2 (does not count as a stitch here or throughout), turn. Place 2 dc in first st. [Sk 2 sts, place (1 sc, 2 dc) in next st]. Repeat inside brackets until 2 sts and the ch 2 turning ch's remain. Sk 2 sts, place 1 sc in the 2nd ch of the ch 2 turning chs. _(36)_

_If it 's difficult to work into the 2nd ch of the turning ch to end the row, you can work around the chains instead._

**Rows 3 through 28:** – Repeat Row 2 using Color A.

In last stitch of Row 28, change to Color B.

**Rows 29 through 42:** – Repeat Row 2 using Color B.

In last stitch of Row 42, change to Color C.

**Rows 43 through 180:** – Repeat Row 2 using Color C.

In last stitch of Row 180, change to Color B.

**Rows 181 through 194:** – Repeat Row 2 using Color B.

In last stitch of Row 194, change to Color A.

**Rows 195 through 222:** – Repeat Row 2 with Color A.

Cut yarn, tie off and weave ends.

## Fringe

To finish off this easy crochet scarf, we'll add classic fringe. You can leave the fringe off if you'd like!

First, you'll need to decide 2 things:

  1. How long you'd like the fringe to be

  2. How many strands of yarn to place in each stitch for your fringe. The more strands of yarn you put in each stitch for the fringe, the more full your fringe will be.




For example: 1) 4 inch fringe. 2) 2 strands per stitch. This means you would need 72 strands per edge or 144 strands total.

Once you've decided on these 2 requirements, you'll cut strands of yarn approximately 2 times longer than your desired fringe length.

For example: My fringe is 4 inches long when finished. So I cut my strands of yarn to be about 8+ inches.

Using something hard (like a book) for an approximate measurement works well. You can wrap the yarn around this item and then cut on one side to create multiple long strands at once.

Take 2 strands (or however many you've decided to use per stitch) and fold them in half so you have a loop. Keep the ends of the strands even. Use your hook to pull the loop through the stitch on the short end of the scarf. Then, pull the tails through the loop and pull on the tails to tighten them.

Repeat along the short ends of your scarf to finish the fringe. Once the fringe is completed, you're all done. Enjoy!
